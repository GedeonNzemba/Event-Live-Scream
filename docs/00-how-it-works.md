# 00 — How it works, in plain language

*Start here. Everything else in this repository is detail underneath this story.*

---

## The one-line version

> Sylvain pays €39. A trained young person in Brazzaville turns up at his family's wedding
> with a camera, a charged battery pack and a data bundle Sylvain paid for — so nobody in
> the family has to give up their phone, their charge, or their afternoon. Sylvain watches
> from Paris, says a word into the room during the toast, and gets the complete recording
> that evening.

That's it. The rest is making that reliable on a Congolese network.

---

## The cast

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Sylvain**, 34 | Lives in Créteil, near Paris. Works in logistics. Hasn't been home in four years |
| **Maman Clarisse**, 61 | His mother, in Makélékélé, Brazzaville. Owns the good phone in the house |
| **Grace**, 27 | His niece. It's her wedding |
| **Merveille**, 23 | Lives two neighbourhoods over. Unemployed, owns a decent Android, free on Saturdays. He is about to become an Elongo *correspondant* |
| **Eight more relatives** | Brussels, London, Montréal, Atlanta, Johannesburg, and three more in France |

---

## Today, without us

Sylvain calls his mother on the morning of the wedding.

> **Sylvain:** Maman, you'll call me during the ceremony?
> **Clarisse:** *(sighs)* There was no current yesterday. The phone is at 22%. And I need it —
> everyone is calling me today, it's Grace's wedding.
> **Sylvain:** Just ten minutes, when they exchange the rings.
> **Clarisse:** I'll try. Ask your cousin, maybe he'll hold it.

What actually happens: a 6-minute call at 15:40 from someone's pocket, mostly ceiling and
noise, cutting three times. Sylvain sees about ninety seconds of his niece's wedding. There
is **no recording**. The moment is gone permanently.

Meanwhile his cousin in Brussels made the same request. So did his sister in Montréal. Each
one was a separate imposition on the same exhausted woman, and each was refused for the same
reasons.

## With Elongo, step by step

### Step 1 — Somebody books it (Tuesday, 4 taps, inside WhatsApp)

Sylvain doesn't download anything. He messages the Elongo WhatsApp number:

```
  Sylvain →   Mariage de ma nièce, samedi, Makélékélé, Brazzaville

  Elongo  →   Samedi 14 mars. What time does it start?

  Sylvain →   14h, until about 18h

  Elongo  →   That's a Cérémonie — up to 8 hours, live + full recording
              + highlights + 30 photos.

              €89.

              Or share this link with the family and split it:
              elongo.cg/grace-thierry
```

**No app install. No account. No password.** Congolese families already live in WhatsApp —
it's where funerals get organised and money gets collected. Fighting that is how this
company dies.

### Step 2 — The family pool fills up (Tuesday evening)

Sylvain drops the link into the family group. This is the part that already happens in these
families for funerals and dowries; we're just attaching a camera to it.

```
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  Mariage de Grace & Thierry                │
  │  Samedi 14 mars, 14h00 · Makélékélé        │
  ├────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │  €89 total                                 │
  │  ██████████████████████████████░░░  €80/89 │
  │                                            │
  │  Sylvain      (Paris)      €15  ✓          │
  │  Bernadette   (Bruxelles)  €10  ✓          │
  │  Christian    (Montréal)   €10  ✓          │
  │  Prisca       (Londres)    €10  ✓          │
  │  + 4 autres                €35  ✓          │
  │                                            │
  │  €9 left · [ Contribuer ]                  │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Nine relatives, €10 each. **Nobody paid €89.** And eight people who had never heard of
Elongo just used it, introduced by a trusted relative, at a moment when they cared. That is
our entire marketing budget for this event: zero.

### Step 3 — Merveille gets the job (Wednesday)

His phone buzzes:

```
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  NEW PRESENCE                              │
  │  Sat 14 Mar · 14:00–18:00 · Makélékélé     │
  │  Cérémonie · wedding                       │
  │                                            │
  │  You earn      20 000 XAF  (~€30)          │
  │  Transport     4 000 XAF                   │
  │  Data bundle   paid by Elongo ✓            │
  │                                            │
  │  [ Accept ]        [ Decline ]             │
  └────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

He accepts. Thursday, 6 GB lands on his SIM — **we buy it, never him.** The moment a
correspondent is out of pocket for data, the next gig stops being worth answering, and the
supply side dies quietly without anyone complaining.

### Step 4 — Saturday, 13:30 — arrival

Merveille arrives thirty minutes early with the kit: his phone, a 20,000 mAh power bank, a
small gimbal, a clip-on microphone, and a badge everyone can see.

He greets the head of the family first — always — and walks through a short checklist on the
phone, out loud:

```
  ☑  Head of family consents to filming            Papa Théo — 13:41
  ☑  May we film the church ceremony?              yes
  ☑  May we film the dot negotiation?              NO — family only
  ☑  May we film children other than the couple?   with parents present
  ☑  Guests can see the badge                      yes
```

That third line matters. A *dot* negotiation between two families is private. An operator
who films it doesn't just make a bad video — he injures a real family, and word travels
through a diaspora this size in a week.

**The family's word beats the paying customer's, always.** If Papa Théo says stop, the camera
stops, even though Sylvain is paying and asking to continue. That's written into Sylvain's
terms too, so it's never a surprise.

### Step 5 — 14:00 — nine people, five countries, one link

Everyone clicks a link and watches in their browser. No install. Sylvain sees the picture,
who else is watching, and — this is the unusual part — an honest status line.

## Step 6 — The part that makes this a company

Here's a real three-hour wedding run through our simulator, on a link that behaves the way
Brazzaville actually behaves. **These are measured outputs, not a mock-up.**

```
 14:00 ─────────── 15:00 ─────────── 16:00 ─────────── 17:00
  ▆▆▇▇▆▇▆▇▇▇▇▇▆▇▇▇▆▆▆▆▆▆▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▃▄▃▃▃▃▃▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▁▃▃▃▃▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▄▆▆▆▆▆▆▆
  └── good ──┘└─ guests arrive, ─┘└ BLACKOUT ┘└── recovers, dancing ──┘
              cell congests        20 min
```

**14:00–14:40 — good.** 480p, sometimes 720p. Sylvain sees his niece's dress properly.

**14:40–15:30 — two hundred guests arrive**, all posting photos on the same cell tower. The
uplink collapses to a third. Elongo steps down 480p → 360p → 240p. Sylvain's status line
reads *"Réseau chargé · 240p"*. **The audio never wavers** — and audio is what he actually
came for: the singing, the drums, his mother's voice, his own name called across the room.

**15:30–15:50 — the tower loses power.** Nothing gets through. This is the moment that
decides whether this is a business.

```
  A NORMAL VIDEO CALL              ELONGO
  ─────────────────────            ──────────────────────────────
  Call drops.                      Screen holds the last frame.
                                   Status: "Connexion perdue —
  Somebody at a wedding             enregistrement en cours,
  must notice, find the             vous ne perdez rien"
  phone, and call back.
                                   Merveille keeps filming.
  That took 45 seconds,             Every second goes to the
  22 separate times today.          phone's storage first.

  The 20 minutes are gone           At 15:50 the signal returns.
  forever. Including the            Live resumes. The missing
  speeches.                         20 minutes upload quietly
                                    in the background.
```

**Merveille never stops filming, and doesn't need to know anything is wrong.** The camera
doesn't care whether the network exists — that's the entire point of the architecture.

**15:50–18:00 — recovers** to 360p and 480p for the dancing.

### The battery, which was the whole original complaint

The kit started at 55%. The app knows the booking ends at 18:00, so it does arithmetic no
video call does: *can I reach 18:00 at this quality?* When the answer is no, it turns off the
preview screen first (Merveille is watching the wedding, not the handset) and lowers frame
rate before resolution.

It finished at **9% — with the ceremony complete.** Reaching the end at 360p beats two hours
of 720p followed by a dead phone, every time.

And Maman Clarisse's phone was in her pocket the whole afternoon, fully hers.

## Step 7 — 16:20 — Sylvain speaks

During the toast, Sylvain taps **Parler**. Merveille gets a prompt, accepts, and walks the
camera to the couple.

For 90 seconds the system switches to a real-time connection — the expensive kind, the kind
WhatsApp uses for the entire call. Sylvain's voice comes out of the phone speaker. The room
turns and answers him. Grace waves at the camera.

Then it switches back.

> **This is the core insight of the product.** *Watching* and *talking* are different things.
> WhatsApp charges you the cost of a live conversation for all three hours — including the
> ninety minutes you're just watching people dance and wouldn't notice if the picture were
> eight seconds old. We run the cheap, resilient mode by default and pay for real-time only
> in the ninety seconds that need it. That single split is why a three-hour ceremony survives
> both the network and the battery.

## Step 8 — What everyone ends up with

**By 18:20**, an hour after the wedding ends, the backlog has finished uploading. Everyone
gets:

- the complete recording — **including the twenty blacked-out minutes**
- a 3-minute highlight reel
- 30 photographs
- a link that works for relatives who missed it entirely

**The measured result for that afternoon:**

| | Elongo | A normal video call |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heard it live | **88.9%** | 87.2% |
| Saw it live | **88.6%** | 87.2% |
| **Have it forever** | **100%** | **0%** |
| Times it dropped | 0 | 22 |
| Time lost waiting to be called back | 0 | 16m30s |
| Family phones used | 0 | 1, all afternoon |
| Relatives who had to ask a favour | 0 | 9, separately |

The live columns are close, and we say so honestly. **The last four rows are the business.**

## Why this isn't "just a better WhatsApp"

The four things the family actually said, and what each one costs us to fix:

| What they said | What fixes it |
| --- | --- |
| *"The connection keeps cutting"* | The degradation ladder + store-and-forward |
| *"The phone is at 22%, there was no current"* | **A power bank in the kit.** €18 |
| *"I need my phone"* | **It's Merveille's phone, not hers.** €30 |
| *"I'm busy, ask your cousin"* | **Merveille is paid to hold it.** That's the job |

Three of the four are solved by a person and a battery, not by software. That's why this is a
service business with a hard technical core, and why a better video codec was never going to
be the answer.
